Guide to buying property in Indonesia

What to check before you pay, from title types to the title transfer. This is general guidance, not legal advice. Always use a PPAT or notary for the transaction.

Know the types of land title

The title type decides who may own the property, for how long, and how easily it can be resold or used as bank collateral.

SHM (Hak Milik, freehold)
The strongest, full ownership right with no time limit. Only Indonesian citizens and certain entities may hold it.
SHGB (Hak Guna Bangunan, right to build)
The right to build on and own buildings on land for a set term, common for developer housing and companies. The term can be extended and renewed. For Indonesian homeowners, SHGB can usually be upgraded to SHM.
Hak Pakai (right of use)
A right to use land for a set term. This is the title foreigners can hold for a residence, under certain conditions.
SHM Sarusun (strata title)
Ownership certificate for an apartment unit, including a share of the common areas and land.
Girik, Petok D, letter C
Not certificates, but proof of tax payment on customary land. They must be registered at the Land Office before they can be safely sold or mortgaged.
Leasehold
A long-term lease recorded in a notarial deed, common in Bali. The buyer holds the lease for the agreed term, not ownership of the land.

Check the paperwork before you pay

  • Check the certificate at the Land Office (BPN) through a PPAT/notary. This confirms the certificate is genuine, the holder's name matches, and it is not blocked, seized or mortgaged.
  • Match the physical land: size, boundaries and position on site must match the certificate plan. Ask for a re-survey if in doubt.
  • Check the spatial zoning (RTRW/RDTR) on the ATR/BPN GISTARU website or with the local planning office, so your intended use fits, for example residential, tourism or commercial.
  • Make sure land tax (PBB) is paid up to the current year. Ask for the SPPT and payment receipts.
  • For buildings, ask for the PBG (building approval, which replaced the IMB) and the SLF if available.
  • Make sure the seller is entitled to sell. For inherited land, all heirs must agree. If the seller is married, the spouse's consent is usually required.

How a sale works

  1. Site visit and negotiationSee the site in person and check road access, water, electricity and the surroundings.
  2. Booking depositPay the deposit against a receipt stating the property, price and settlement deadline.
  3. Certificate checkDone by the PPAT at the Land Office before any deed is signed.
  4. PPJBA conditional sale agreement, used when paying in stages or while documents are still being processed.
  5. AJB before a PPATThe deed of sale is signed after BPHTB and the seller's income tax are paid.
  6. Title transferThe PPAT registers the transfer at the Land Office until the certificate is in the buyer's name.

Buying with a mortgage (KPR)

  • Down payment is set by the bank and Bank Indonesia policy. Some programmes offer low down payments for first homes.
  • Fixed vs floating rate: fixed-rate promotions usually last only the first few years. Ask for the expected floating rate afterwards, because that sets your long-term instalment.
  • Healthy instalment: ideally no more than about 30% of monthly income.
  • Resale property: the bank will appraise it. The valuation can be lower than the price, and the gap adds to your down payment.

Foreign buyers

Foreigners cannot hold land under freehold (Hak Milik). The legal options are:

  • Hak Pakai (right of use) on a landed house or apartment unit as a residence, subject to a minimum price set per region.
  • Through a PT PMA (foreign-owned company) holding HGB for business use.
  • Leasehold (long-term lease) through a notarial deed.

Avoid nominee arrangements, where land is in an Indonesian's name but paid for and controlled by a foreigner. Such agreements are not valid under Indonesian land law and put the property at risk.

Land area units

1 are = 100 m² and 1 hectare = 100 are = 10,000 m². The are is widely used for land deals in Bali and parts of Java, including for prices, for example "Rp 500 million per are". Every land listing on Nusantara Property shows size in m² and are.
